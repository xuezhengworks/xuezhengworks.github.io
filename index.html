About

I am a post-candidacy Ph.D. candidate in Electrical and Computer Engineering at The Ohio State University, advised by Prof. Aylin Yener in the INSPIRE@OhioState Lab.

My research develops learning methods for institutions and edge devices that need to collaborate while keeping local data and model information private. I work across algorithms, theory, and systems evaluation.

Research interests

  1. Federated & decentralized learning

    Collaborative learning across heterogeneous clients without centralizing their data.

  2. Privacy-preserving collaboration

    Secure aggregation and peer-to-peer methods that protect local information.

  3. Performative learning

Research 01

Foundation models · Federated learning · Edge AI

Federated Foundation Models at the Edge

My ongoing work explores how pretrained foundation models, including large language models and mixture-of-experts architectures, can be collaboratively trained, adapted, and deployed across distributed edge clients without centralizing their local data.

Related publications

Server-Side Sparse Assignment for Federated Mixture-of-Experts

X. Zheng and A. Yener

Accepted to IEEE Information Theory Workshop, 2026

This work studies federated mixture-of-experts learning under partial expert visibility, where resource-constrained clients can access only a subset of experts. S3-MoE estimates client-specific expert relevance on the server and uses an exploit–explore strategy to assign effective sparse expert subsets.

Research 02

Distribution shifts · Robust optimization

Performative and evolving federated learning

Deployed models can alter the data they later observe. This line of work studies robust federated optimization and decentralized learning over changing interaction graphs when model deployment, network evolution, and data shifts are tightly coupled.

Research 03

Personalization · Secure aggregation

Private peer-to-peer collaboration without a central server

Personalized agents learn whom to collaborate with while keeping individual models private. The work combines directed collaboration graphs, clustering, and Shamir secret sharing for practical secure aggregation in peer-to-peer networks.
